# Maya.exe — Short-Form Batch 01

Purpose: launch Maya.exe with useful, repeatable beauty-tech content that can later route viewers to verified TikTok Shop products, software affiliates, digital products, or services. Maya is always disclosed as a virtual AI creator. Do not claim Maya personally used or tested a product unless a real documented test exists.

## Publishing rules

- Format: 20–35 seconds unless the idea needs more explanation.
- First 1.5 seconds: show the result/problem before the explanation.
- One video = one question.
- End CTA should point to the useful next step, not force a purchase.
- TikTok Shop links stay off until the exact product listing, current price, and affiliate destination are verified.
- For health/skin claims, avoid diagnosis and verify claims with qualified sources.

## 01 — AI chooses the whole makeup look

**Hook:** “I gave AI one sentence and told it to choose the whole look.”

**Visual beats:** Maya face close-up → prompt on phone → three suggested looks → final chosen look card.

**Script:**
“Soft glam, five minutes, work-safe, and nothing complicated. I gave that exact brief to an AI beauty tool. It gave me three directions. This one is the most realistic because it keeps the eye simple and puts the color in the lip. The useful part isn’t letting AI decide everything — it’s using it to narrow the choices before you spend money.”

**CTA:** “I’m collecting the beauty AI tools worth testing at Maya.exe.”

**Monetization test:** AI beauty software affiliate.

## 02 — Can virtual try-on stop a bad purchase?

**Hook:** “This is the only reason I care about virtual makeup try-on.”

**Visual beats:** lipstick grid → try-on mockup → two shades rejected → one finalist.

**Script:**
“It doesn’t need to predict reality perfectly. If it can help me remove four obviously wrong shades before checkout, it already saved time. I’d still check real swatches, lighting, and the return policy. AI should reduce bad options — not pretend it knows your face better than you do.”

**CTA:** “Full try-on checklist is going on Maya.exe.”

**Monetization test:** beauty app affiliate + guide lead-in.

## 03 — Beauty gadget: worth it or gimmick?

**Hook:** “Before I buy any beauty gadget, it has to survive these four questions.”

**Visual beats:** product silhouette → four on-screen questions.

**Script:**
“One: what exact job does it do? Two: is there a subscription or app dependency? Three: what happens if the app disappears? Four: can I return it after actually trying it? If the product page makes those hard to answer, I don’t care how futuristic it looks.”

**CTA:** “I made the full beauty-tech buying checklist at Maya.exe.”

**Monetization test:** TikTok Shop/product affiliate readiness.

## 04 — AI vs human makeup brief

**Hook:** “AI gave the prettier answer. The human answer was more useful.”

**Visual beats:** split screen “AI” / “human” → two brief cards.

**Script:**
“I asked for a five-minute office makeup routine. AI suggested a complete polished look. A human would probably ask the annoying but important question first: what are you already wearing and what products do you actually own? That’s the difference. AI is fast. Good judgment adds context.”

**CTA:** “More human-vs-AI beauty tests are coming.”

**Monetization test:** audience growth; no direct sale needed.

## 06 — AI skincare advice: where I stop trusting it

**Hook:** “AI can organize a skincare routine. This is where I stop listening.”

**Visual beats:** routine planner → red line → medical/diagnostic language crossed out.

**Script:**
“I’m fine using AI to sort products, explain basic terms, or help me prepare questions. The second it starts diagnosing a skin condition or making medical promises, that answer needs a qualified professional and real evidence. Convenience is not the same thing as clinical judgment.”

**CTA:** “I put my AI skincare safety rules on Maya.exe.”

**Monetization test:** trust-building content.

## 07 — The €30 makeup routine challenge

**Hook:** “Can AI build a full makeup routine under €30 without making nonsense choices?”

**Visual beats:** €30 budget → category list → total counter → problem flags.

**Script:**
“I gave AI a hard budget and five required categories. The interesting part isn’t whether it hits €29.99. It’s whether the products are actually available, whether the shades make sense, and whether it sneaks in fake prices. That’s what I’m checking before I call the list useful.”

**CTA:** “I’ll post the verified version when every price is real.”

**Monetization test:** future product affiliate roundup.

## 08 — Smart mirror: what would make me buy one?

**Hook:** “A smart mirror needs to do more than put an app on a mirror.”

**Visual beats:** mirror concept → brightness → color temperature → camera/privacy → app dependence.

**Script:**
“For me, the useful features would be consistent lighting, adjustable color temperature, a normal mirror when the tech is off, and no creepy data collection just to turn on a light. If the smart features make the basic mirror worse, that’s not smart beauty tech.”

**CTA:** “Smart mirror test criteria are now in the Maya shop queue.”

**Monetization test:** TikTok Shop category test.

## 09 — I built a beauty app without coding

**Hook:** “This started as one sentence: ‘Help me choose what makeup to buy.’”

**Visual beats:** text prompt → simple app screens → checklist result.

**Script:**
“Instead of another generic recommendation page, I’d make the app ask what you already own, your budget, the occasion, and what you refuse to wear. Then it gives a shortlist instead of fifty products. That’s the kind of tiny app AI builders make interesting — not because coding disappeared, but because testing an idea got cheaper.”

**CTA:** “I’m documenting the build on Maya.exe.”

**Monetization test:** AI app-builder affiliate + future micro-SaaS.

## 10 — Don’t buy from the first viral video

**Hook:** “A viral beauty product can still be a terrible buy for you.”

**Visual beats:** viral counter → checklist: use case / returns / price / evidence.

**Script:**
“Before I click Shop, I want four things: the job it solves, the current price, the return policy, and whether the claim in the video is actually supported. Viral tells you what got attention. It doesn’t tell you what deserves your money.”

**CTA:** “That’s the rule behind every future Maya.exe shop pick.”

**Monetization test:** trust + TikTok Shop conversion quality.

## Metrics to record per video

Track: views, 3-second hold, average watch time, completion rate, profile visits, site clicks, TikTok Shop clicks when enabled, affiliate clicks, saves, comments, and revenue. Do not scale a format from views alone; scale formats that create both attention and useful downstream actions.
